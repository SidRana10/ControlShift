# ControlShift retrieval benchmark

_Generated by `eval/benchmark.py` from `eval/results.json`; every number below is measured. Labels are AI-curated and source-checked, **not human-reviewed**._

## 1. Methodology

- Corpus: 384 source chunks from NIST CSF 1.1 and 2.0 (430 dense embedding passages; passages are scored as their parent chunk).
- Questions: 40 (39 final, 1 provisional) from `eval/questions.jsonl` (sha256 `c2f52a638ce2d832...`).
- Every mode answers the same questions with the same version filters, the same corpus, the same output depth (top-10) in one process on one machine. Models are loaded once.
- Hybrid: BM25 top-30 + dense top-30 fused with RRF (k=60). Rerank: the top 30 fused candidates are rescored by `cross-encoder/ms-marco-MiniLM-L-6-v2`.
- Dense model `sentence-transformers/all-MiniLM-L6-v2`, search backend `numpy`.
- No ranking parameter, weight or ID boost was tuned on these questions; defaults were fixed before the benchmark was run.

## 3. Relevance-label provenance

- Labels were written by an AI assistant (Claude) by reading the stored source chunks and, for cross-version pairs, the official NIST crosswalk rows. **No retrieval output was used to choose or grade relevance.**
- Chunk IDs, versions, pages, sections and source excerpts were checked against the SQLite database (`controlshift-db`); the test suite re-checks them.
- **No human has reviewed these labels**; they must not be called independently verified. Audit them in `eval/questions_audit.md`.
- 1 query(ies) whose relevance is uncertain are marked `provisional` and excluded from every headline metric (reported separately in section 4b).
- Chunks not listed as relevant are treated as non-relevant, so a retrieved chunk that is actually relevant but unlabelled lowers a score (a known bias, see section 10).

## 4. Metric definitions

- **Recall@k** = relevant chunks in the top k / all relevant chunks for the query.
- **Hit@5** = 1 if at least one relevant chunk is in the top 5.
- **MRR@10** = 1 / rank of the first relevant chunk within the top 10 (0 if none).
- **nDCG@5** with gain 2^grade - 1 (grades 1 and 2), discount 1/log2(rank+1), normalised by the ideal ordering.
- **Version isolation** (queries with a filter only) = share of queries whose returned results were all from the requested version.
- Repeated chunk IDs count once at their first position. Scores are means over final queries; 95% CIs are seeded percentile bootstraps over queries.

## 9. Summary of measured differences

- Highest overall nDCG@5 in this run: **Hybrid RRF + cross-encoder** (0.848). This is a point estimate on 39 queries; check the confidence intervals above before concluding that modes differ.

## 10. Known limitations and threats to validity

- **Small sample**: 39 final queries; per-category cells have 8-11 queries. Confidence intervals are wide.
- **Labels are AI-curated and not human-reviewed**; some judgments (grade 1 vs not relevant) are subjective, and the author of the labels is a language model with possible blind spots.
- **Incomplete labels**: unlabelled chunks count as non-relevant, so correct-but-unlabelled retrievals (e.g. a neighbouring subcategory) are penalised. This affects all modes but not equally.
- **Corpus-specific**: CSF is a small, highly structured corpus (384 chunks); results do not generalise to large corpora or other domains.
- **Question style**: questions were written for this benchmark by the same assistant that built the system's tests; many control-level queries are close paraphrases of control text, which favours lexical overlap.
- **Exact-ID and tie behaviour** of RRF (equal weights, deterministic chunk-ID tie-break) was not tuned; any ID-aware boosting is future work that must be evaluated on a separate held-out set to avoid overfitting to these questions.
- **Latency** is measured on one machine, single-threaded request loop, warm caches; not a load test. Results depend on the device PyTorch selected (see environment).
- Dense passages are scored as parent chunks; sub-passage retrieval quality is not measured separately.
